S-corp savings calculator

As a sole proprietor or single-member LLC, you pay self-employment tax of about 14% on your profit (15.3% on 92.35% of it; the Social Security part stops at $184,500). An S-corp pays you a salary and passes the rest of the profit to you without that tax, but it adds payroll and a separate business return. See which costs less for you.

Self-employment tax is 15.3% of your profit. Payroll taxes are the same 15.3% on your salary only (you and the S-corp each pay half), plus federal unemployment tax. In Illinois the S-corp's state tax includes the 1.5% replacement tax.

What salary works best?

A lower salary saves more but is harder to defend if the IRS asks. There's no set IRS percentage: the salary should be close to what you'd pay someone else to do your job.
